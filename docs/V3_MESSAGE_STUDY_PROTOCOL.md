# V3 prospective fictional-message study

This study tests whether a small, offline bilingual detector can recognize **clear, non-graphic requests for safety-related support** in newly written fictional messages. It does not test grooming detection, determine whether a speaker is a child, diagnose a situation, authorize sharing, or estimate performance on authentic child language. The caller must declare the source role separately. The generator uses no case log, scraped child conversation, disclosure benchmark text, or real person. All message stems are written for this study and use ordinary school, home, and online contexts without explicit abuse details.

The generator defines paired positive and negative stems in Romanian and Ukrainian. Pair members share an everyday context while the reason for support changes. It applies label-neutral chat formatting to each member. The fixed generator produces 1,280 train pairs, 96 development pairs, and 96 test pairs **per language**. Train, development, and test have disjoint stems and family IDs; exact output text and normalized text may not cross splits. This is a **wording-family holdout**, not a human-authored external evaluation. The study owner authored all three banks and knows their design. Repeated formatted variants within one family are correlated; pair counts and Wilson intervals are descriptive only. No synthetic split can establish prevalence, calibration, age inference, or live safety recall.

Before fitting, freeze the generator, split counts, hashes, model candidates, scorer, selection rule, and numeric target in Git. Fit only on train. Select a vectorizer, regularization, and cutoff on development using, in order: (1) the minimum complete-pair count across Romanian and Ukrainian, (2) the sum of complete pairs, (3) the minimum positive recall, (4) the fewest false reviews, and (5) the simpler feature set. The cutoff is a **synthetic development operating point**, not a calibrated probability. Record the selected model bytes and development report before scoring the untouched test exactly once. Do not revise the model or cutoff on that test. A later repair requires a new, prospectively frozen wording family.

The fixed test target is at least **85/96 complete pairs**, **90/96 positive recalls**, and **at most 10/96 false reviews** in **each language**. Also report a fixed high-confidence error count, the always-review and never-review baselines, overlap checks, and per-family errors. A pass would support only transfer among these constructed message families. A failure remains in the ledger. The public command, if promoted, must identify its output as advisory, avoid echoing message text, require the caller's language and source role, and take no external action. A score cannot grant R1 access or bypass G1 human review.

The design responds to the documented risk that synthetic classifiers can learn generator style instead of the intended distinction. [Research on synthetic text classification](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.647.pdf) reports larger gaps for subjective tasks; this protocol therefore limits its claim to the controlled fictional task and holds out whole stems, rather than reporting a random-row split as language validity.

The corrected v1 taxonomy text explicitly excluded realistic invented disclosure wording. This new, owner-requested experiment uses non-graphic fictional messages and does **not** inherit the specialists' approval of that earlier data boundary. The reviewed taxonomy bytes remain unchanged. Native-speaker review of these new message stems has not occurred; no claim about idiomatic quality or real deployment follows from the synthetic score.
