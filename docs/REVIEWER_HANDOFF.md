## The two decisions needed in each language

Ask **two different people** to review the entire six-category taxonomy independently. One should be a fluent Romanian or Ukrainian language specialist; the other should have child-protection practice or research experience and enough command of the same language to judge the taxonomy's wording. A language specialist cannot sign twice in different roles. Each reviewer should identify ambiguous boundaries, unnatural or legally loaded terms, unsupported age or risk claims, and concrete edits. In particular, ask about Romanian *tutore* and Ukrainian *піклувальник* in R1. The [terminology audit](ROLE_TERMINOLOGY_AUDIT.md) records why those words need scrutiny.

Only after the taxonomy text is settled, invite two fluent reviewers **per language** to independently annotate a new set of permitted abstract D1/S1 cards. A taxonomy reviewer may also annotate if the owner records that overlap; the two annotators of a given language must still be different people. This is a separate agreement study, not a proxy for live-message accuracy. Do not ask anyone to supply real cases, child utterances, plausible paraphrases, grooming dialogue, or reply text. If a reviewer cannot decide from an abstract card, `unknown` is a valid result.

## Invitation text for the owner to adapt and send

> I am seeking an independent review of a Romanian/Ukrainian taxonomy for a child-safety AI research toolkit. The review concerns six short category definitions and their boundaries, especially language naturalness, child-protection framing, and guardian-role terminology. It does not involve real child messages, case files, or intervention decisions. Could your institution suggest a qualified reviewer who is independent of the project? I can provide the exact taxonomy, a focused review form, expected scope, and handling instructions before any commitment. The review will be recorded only with the person's consent and attributed as agreed.

For an annotation invitation, replace the middle sentence with: “The task is to mark enumerated facts on abstract, fictional research cards in a blinded packet; uncertain items may be marked unknown. The cards contain no real or realistic child statements.” Explain the time and compensation terms before a person accepts; neither is promised here. The owner should document who reviewed, their qualifications, any conflicts, what materials they saw, when they returned a decision, and whether permission was given to publish attribution. Store contact details outside Git.

## Once reviewers return

1. Preserve each taxonomy review separately. Resolve proposed wording changes with the reviewers, then record acceptance against the final digest. The two pending JSON files stay pending until the actual decisions satisfy the [review gate](REVIEW_GATE.md).
2. Freeze the model weights, prompt, cutoff, inference code, evaluator, and numeric target **before either author receives an assignment or creates test cards**. Commit the record and its named bytes, then pass it to `roguard-author-kit new`. The [chronology addendum](STUDY_CHRONOLOGY_ADDENDUM.md) explains why freezing after card creation is too late, even if labels remain hidden.
3. Create a newly authored, language-native abstract batch under the [independent intake contract](INDEPENDENT_BATCH_INTAKE.md) and [methodology](METHODOLOGY.md). The [offline author kit](AUTHOR_KIT.md) assigns each author 24 balanced pairs and assembles a private candidate while keeping unreviewed cards outside Git. A separate content reviewer checks that each abstract pair really changes the stated factor and approves the exact bytes after checking the no-realistic-content rule. Seal the batch locally, then run `roguard-blind-packets` and distribute each packet separately. **Do not push the labeled batch yet.** Do not expose author-intended labels, model scores, or the other person's answers during annotation.
4. Give the model operator only the shuffled `reviewer-a.jsonl` packet, without the source batch, owner map, or judgments; [`roguard-predict-bilingual`](BILINGUAL_BASELINE_PREDICTION.md) reads that packet and writes opaque-ID decisions for any of the four registered RO/UK D1/S1 comparator cells. Commit and push the prediction file **before** the labeled batch files and before opening adjudicated labels. The [prediction-seal guide](STUDY_PREDICTION_SEAL.md) gives the exact order and a Git-history verifier. Let each annotator collect content-free answers with `roguard-annotate-packet` on their own packet, keeping each stream and its private `.packet.json` binding outside Git. Each annotator returns both files together. Run `roguard-review-audit` to check packet binding, answer alignment, and agreement. Resolve disagreements while still blind to model output; preserve both originals, their bindings, and any separate adjudication.
5. Immediately after sealing predictions and before opening adjudicated labels, the study owner should run `roguard-replay-bilingual` to reproduce every submitted decision from the registered adapter. Run `roguard-human-eval-registered` only after the two original streams and a separate adjudication file are complete; it binds predictions to the packet, approved source, and registered model identifier. Report per-language counts, uncertainty, false reviews, precision, recall, and the limits of abstract-card evidence. The software records hashes but cannot prove the human study chronology or operator separation. A fluent review or a high synthetic score never certifies detection of authentic child language.
